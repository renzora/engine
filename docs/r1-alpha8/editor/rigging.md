# Rigging & Posing

A rigged model has a skeleton: bones that its mesh follows. In the [Animation workspace](/docs/r1-alpha8/editor/animation#the-animation-workspace) you give a model a skeleton in **Rig** mode, check and paint how its mesh follows the bones in **Skin**, sculpt blend shapes in **Shapes**, and pose and key it in **Animate**, all on the stage.

## Giving a model a skeleton

Put the model on the stage (pick it in the stage's dropdown, or select it in the scene). A model with no skeleton opens in Rig mode.

1. Stand the character in a T or A pose (the model as imported).
2. In the left panel, choose **Humanoid**.
3. Click **Place Points**, then click the model at each point in turn: chin, shoulder, elbow, wrist, groin, knee, ankle. One side is enough; the other is mirrored, and its points show faded. Click a point's row to place it again; `Esc` stops. A point lands in the middle of the part under the cursor, halfway between where the click enters the mesh and where it comes out, so an elbow clicked from the front sits inside the arm rather than on its skin.
4. Once all seven are placed, the stage shows the skeleton they will build, dashed.
5. Click **Generate Skeleton**.

The skeleton has Mixamo's bone names and orientations, so Mixamo clips, and any clip made for a Mixamo rig, play on it as they are. The skin weights are generated by distance to each bone measured across the skin, not through the air, so an arm resting against the ribs or a finger beside the next does not pull skin it only touches.

The fingers are found from the shape of each hand, with no extra points: every finger gets Mixamo's four bones from knuckle to tip (`LeftHandIndex1` to `LeftHandIndex4`, and so on for the thumb, middle, ring and pinky), so a clip that curls the fingers curls them here too. Fingers modelled touching, and jointed fingers built from separate segments, are still told apart. A hand modelled as a mitten, with nothing between the fingers, gets no finger bones and moves as one piece.

The model's file is rewritten with the skeleton and skin in it, and every copy of it in your scenes updates. The original is kept beside it as `<name>.unrigged.glb`, and **Remove Rig** puts it back. Rigging again always starts from that original.

For a head, choose **Face** instead: see [Facial Animation](/docs/r1-alpha8/editor/facial-animation).

### Animals: Quadruped

Choose **Quadruped** for anything on four legs. Stand it on all fours, look at it from the side (**Side**), and place thirteen points: the nose, the base of the skull, the withers (the top of the shoulders), one front leg (shoulder, elbow, wrist, toes), the pelvis (the top of the hips), one hind leg (hip, knee, hock, toes) and the tip of the tail. The far legs are mirrored. For an animal with no tail, put the tail tip at the end of the spine and no tail bones are made.

The skeleton has a pelvis, three spine bones, a chest, two neck bones, a head, a shoulder blade and three leg bones for each front leg, three for each hind leg, and four tail bones. Every bone points along its length (+Y towards its child), so bending a joint is a turn about its X or Z.

### Things that bend: Chain

Choose **Chain** for a tail, a rope, a tentacle, a plant stem, or a shape from the library. Set how many **Bones**, then either place the root end and the tip, or place none and the chain runs along the model's longest side, from its lower end. Each joint is shared smoothly between the bones on either side of it, so the mesh curves there instead of creasing.

A **library shape** (a capsule, a cylinder) can be given a chain too. It has no model file for a skeleton to be written into, so Build Chain first writes its mesh to `models/shapes/<name>.glb` and turns the shape into an instance of that model, keeping its name, place and every other component. Undo puts the shape back. To add shapes ready to bend, tick **Bendable** in the Shape Library: every shape added while it is ticked, clicked in or dragged into the viewport, arrives with a four-bone chain.

### Editing a skeleton by hand

The **Skeleton** section below the templates edits any model's skeleton, or starts one on a model with none (it begins with a single root bone). Click **Edit Skeleton**, then:

- Click a bone in the list or on the stage to select it.
- **Add Child** adds a bone below the selected one, carrying on in its direction.
- **Delete** removes the selected bone; its children hang from its parent.
- **Pick Parent**, then a click on another bone, hangs the selected bone from it.
- **Place Joint**, then a click on the model, puts the selected joint in the middle of the part under the cursor. The X, Y and Z fields set it exactly.
- Type in the name field to rename the selected bone.

Nothing changes until **Apply Skeleton**, which rigs the model's original again with the edited bones and makes fresh weights for them (moving a joint changes which skin is nearest which bone). Bones that moved are turned to keep pointing at their children. **Cancel** throws the edits away. An imported skeleton is edited the same way: its old skin is taken off the original and the edited bones put in its place, and **Remove Rig** still restores the file as it was imported.

### Exporting a rigged model

**Export with Clips**, in the Skeleton section, writes the model on the stage to `exports/<name>.glb`: its meshes, skeleton and skin, and every clip on its animator as a glTF animation, for opening in other tools. Clips are sampled 30 times a second from the curves the engine plays, so eased and stepped keys look the same elsewhere. Muted tracks are left out.

### Reading the skeleton

The right panel lists the skeleton as a tree. Click a bone there or on the stage to select it; the **Bone** tab shows its name, its parent, its length and its partner on the other side.

## Skin: how the mesh follows the bones

Switch to **Skin** to see and paint the weights: how much each bone moves each part of the mesh. The model turns into a heat map of the bone being painted, dark blue where it has no pull through to red where it has all of it. Nothing shows on the scene's model until you save.

<!-- screenshot: skin_mode.png - Skin mode: a forearm painted red to blue on the stage, the brush circle over it, the elbow bent by the test slider -->

- **Auto Weights** weights every vertex by how close it is to each bone, measured across the mesh's surface so skin that is near a bone only through the air (the chest seen from the upper arm) is left alone. **Smooth** blends across the mesh; **Sharp** keeps hard edges between bones.
- **The brush** paints the chosen bone. Pick the bone in the **Paint Bone** list, or Ctrl+click it on the model.

| Brush | Does |
|---|---|
| Add | Pulls the vertices towards the bone |
| Remove | Pushes them off it, onto the bones around |
| Smooth | Evens the weight out with the vertices around it |
| Set | Sets it to the Strength exactly |

  **Radius** is a share of the model's size, **Strength** how hard each stroke presses, and **Falloff** how it fades to the brush's edge. **Mirror strokes** paints the other side's bone at the mirrored spot at the same time. Each stroke is one undo step.
- **Select** (switch from **Paint** at the top of the left panel) fixes weights exactly where automatic ones guessed wrong. Click a part of the model to select the whole piece under the cursor (one tooth, an eyeball, a buckle, a hat), or drag a box to select every vertex inside it, through the model. Shift adds to the selection and Ctrl takes away; **All**, **None**, **Invert** and **Grow** adjust it. Selected vertices show pale over their heat. Then, with the bone picked in **Paint Bone**:
  - **Bind Rigidly to Bone** gives the selection wholly to that bone, so it moves as one solid piece with it. This is how a part is attached to a bone: the teeth to the jaw, a helmet to the head.
  - **Set Weight** gives the selection the chosen share of the bone, the rest staying with the bones it had.
  - **Auto Weights** weights only the selection, leaving the rest as it is.

  Each is one undo step, saved into the model like painting.
- **Test** bends the painted bone with the Bend sliders, or **Range of Motion** swings every joint, so you see the weights working as you paint. You paint the mesh as it is bent. Only the stage moves; nothing is keyed.
- **Checks** counts vertices no bone moves and vertices whose weights do not add up to 1; **Normalise** fixes the second.
- **Save Weights** writes the weights into the model file and reloads it everywhere. Leaving Skin saves anything painted; **Revert** throws it away.

Weights are saved straight into the `.glb`. A model the engine had to rebuild on load (one without normals, which gets flat ones by splitting its vertices) cannot be saved this way; the Console says which mesh.

## Sculpting blend shapes

A blend shape (morph target) is a second shape for the mesh, a smile, a blink, a vowel, a bulge, that the mesh can be dialled toward by a weight from 0 to 1. Switch to **Shapes** to make them by sculpting the model on the stage. It works on any model, with or without a skeleton, and needs no other modelling tool.

<!-- screenshot: shapes_mode.png - Shapes mode: a head on the stage with the corners of the mouth sculpted up into a smile, the brush ring on the cheek, the shape list on the left with Smile picked -->

The model stands at rest (its bind pose) while you sculpt, which is the shape a blend shape is stored against. Nothing shows on the scene's model until you save.

- **Blend Shapes** lists the model's shapes, the ones it came with and the ones you made, each with a preview slider. Click the circle beside a shape to sculpt it. Type a name and press **New Shape** to make one; it starts flat (no change) at full weight. **Preview at Rest** puts every other slider back to zero.
- **The brush** sculpts the picked shape:

| Brush | Does |
|---|---|
| Draw | Pushes the surface out (Ctrl pulls it in) |
| Smooth | Relaxes bumps toward the surface around them (or hold Shift with any brush) |
| Grab | Drags the surface under the brush with the cursor, for big moves like opening a jaw |
| Inflate | Swells the surface along its own facing (Ctrl shrinks it) |
| Flatten | Presses the surface flat |
| Pinch | Draws the surface toward the brush's centre (Ctrl spreads it) |

  **Radius** is a share of the model's size (`[` and `]` change it), **Strength** how hard each dab presses, and **Falloff** how it fades to the edge. **Mirror X** sculpts the other side of the model at the same time, so a smile comes out even; along the middle of the face the two sides meet and stay on the centre line. A stroke only moves the mesh it starts on, so sculpting the lips leaves the teeth behind them alone; turn on **Every mesh** to sculpt across meshes (the lids and the lashes on them together). Each stroke is one undo step.
- You sculpt the surface as you see it. A stroke shows its shape at full weight, and any other shape you have dialled in stays in, so a corrective shape can be sculpted on top of the pose it corrects.
- **Shape** (right) shows the shape being sculpted, with its **Preview weight**. **Rename** gives it the name typed in the name field, **Clear** flattens it back to nothing, **Delete** removes it. Imported shapes can be sculpted, renamed and deleted like your own.
- **Save Shapes** writes every shape into the model file and reloads it everywhere. Leaving Shapes saves anything sculpted; **Revert** throws it away.

Shapes are saved into the `.glb` as glTF morph targets: how far each vertex moves, and how its shading turns, worked out from the sculpted surface. Shapes you did not touch keep exactly the data they had. A shape lives on every mesh it moves, so "Blink" sculpted across the lids and the lashes is one shape in the list and one slider per mesh among the blend shape sliders. The first save keeps the model as it was beside it, as `<name>.unshaped.glb`. A model the engine had to rebuild on load cannot be saved this way; the Console says which mesh.

Once saved, the new shapes are ordinary blend shapes: they show as sliders in Animate's **Controls** tab and key like any other (see [Blend shapes](/docs/r1-alpha8/editor/facial-animation#blend-shapes)). A clip keys a shape by its number, so deleting a shape moves the ones after it up a place, and a clip keyed on them then drives the next shape along; new shapes are always added after the existing ones, so making one never disturbs a clip.

## Posing a bone

In **Animate**, click a bone on the stage. A bone is drawn from its joint to the next, and belongs to the joint it turns around, so clicking the forearm selects the forearm. Shift+click adds a bone to the selection.

- **Turn** it: `E`, then drag a ring. The white ring turns it about the view.
- **Move** it: `W`, then drag an arrow, or the centre square to move it in the plane facing you.
- **Local** or **World** in the header sets whether the gizmo follows the bone's own axes or the world's.
- The **Pose** tab on the right has the selected bone's turn as three sliders.

Each drag is one undo step.

### A pose lasts until the playhead moves

While a clip is playing in the timeline, each frame of it sets the bones it animates. A pose you make sits on top of that until you **key** it or move the playhead, then it is dropped and the clip's pose shows again, the same as in other animation tools.

## Keying

Press `K` with the cursor over the stage or the timeline, or **Key Pose** in the Pose tab, to key every bone you have posed and every selected bone at the playhead. A bone with no track in the clip gets one. The key plays at once; the clip file is saved a moment later.

With **Auto key** on (the chip on the stage, or Record in the timeline), every change you make to a bone is keyed as you make it.

Bone keys go to the bone's own track whether you key from the stage, the Pose tab or the Inspector's keyframe button.

## Pose tools

These act on the selected bones, or on every bone when none is selected.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| Reset to Rest | Puts the bones back in the pose the model was rigged in |
| Mirror | Copies each selected left bone's pose to the right one, or the other way round |
| Flip Pose | Swaps the whole pose left for right, the other half of a walk |
| Copy / Paste | Takes a pose from one frame to another |
| Paste Mirrored | Pastes the copied pose flipped left for right |

Mirroring works from each bone's rest pose, so it is right for any rig whose two sides mirror each other, whatever way its bones are pointed. Left and right bones are paired by name: `LeftArm`/`RightArm`, `arm.L`/`arm.R` and `arm_l`/`arm_r` all work.

## The pose library

The left panel's **Pose** tab has the **Pose Library**, which keeps whole poses by name. Type a name and **Save Pose** saves the pose on the stage; click a saved pose to put it back (on the selected bones, or all of them), and key it as usual. Poses are files in the project's `animations/poses/` folder, one `.pose` each, so every scene shares them, and a pose made on one Mixamo character fits any other.

## Controls

A control is a slider you make yourself: a named pose of bones and blend shapes that the slider dials in from the rest pose. A curl for a tail, a fist for a hand, a door's swing, a sneer for a face: any rig, any pose. They live in the left panel's **Controls** tab in **Animate**.

### Making one

1. Pose the model on the stage (the gizmo, the Pose tab, the pose tools) and set any blend shapes in the **Blend Shapes** list under the controls.
2. Type a name and click **Save as Control**.

The control records how far each bone is from its rest pose (bones that have not moved are left out, so a control never pins the rest of the rig) and the weight of every blend shape that is on. Start from the rest pose (**Reset to Rest**) so the control holds only the change you made. Its slider then runs from 0 (rest) to 1 (the pose you saved), easing each bone along one arc between the two.

### Using them

Drag the sliders to pose the model. Controls add together: rotations combine, moves and scale changes add, and blend shape weights add (held between 0 and 1). The pose behaves like any other: it [lasts until the playhead moves](#a-pose-lasts-until-the-playhead-moves), then the sliders go back to rest.

- **Key Controls** keys every bone and blend shape the sliders you moved affect, at the playhead; with none moved, it keys everything any control affects, as it is now. `K` over the stage keys them too. Keys play at once, and `Ctrl+Z` takes them back.
- **Reset Controls** puts everything the sliders moved back to rest.

### Editing one

Click **Edit** beside a slider:

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| Name, **Rename** | Renames the control |
| Min, Max | The slider's range. Past 1 pushes the pose further; below 0 makes it signed |
| **Re-capture** | Replaces the control's pose with the one on the stage |
| **Capture Negative End** | Saves the pose on the stage as what the slider does at -1, so a signed slider is two poses (a brow that raises one way and knits the other). Sets Min to -1 if it was 0 |
| **Clear Negative End** | Drops that pose: below zero the slider then plays the positive pose reversed, which suits a look left / right |
| **Delete** | Removes the control |

### The file

A model's controls are saved beside it as `<model>.controls` (`head.glb` gets `head.controls`), so every scene using the model shares them and they go into version control with it. It is plain RON text, one entry per control:

```ron
(
    version: 1,
    controls: [
        (
            name: "Tail Curl",
            min: 0.0,
            max: 1.0,
            targets: [
                Bone(bone: "tail_1", rotate: (35.0, 0.0, 0.0)),
                Bone(bone: "tail_2", rotate: (40.0, 0.0, 0.0)),
                BlendShape(mesh: "body", name: "tailFluff", weight: 0.6),
            ],
        ),
    ],
)
```

A `Bone` target has `rotate` (degrees about the bone's own X, Y, Z, after its rest rotation), `translate` (added to its rest position) and `scale` (added to its rest scale); any can be left out. A `BlendShape` names the mesh node and the shape, by `name` where the mesh names its shapes and by `index` otherwise. A control can also have a `negative` list (the -1 end) and a `default` (where the slider sits before it is touched). Fields that are left out take their defaults, so a hand-written file can be as short as the example.

A face rig is offered its built-in expressions as a preset of controls; see [Facial Animation](/docs/r1-alpha8/editor/facial-animation#expressions).

## IK

Inverse kinematics makes an arm or a leg reach. The **Limbs** section lists the rig's limbs (each hand or foot with an elbow or knee above it). With a limb's IK on, move its hand or foot (`W`) and the elbow or knee bends so the hand stays on the end of the arm, instead of the wrist stretching away. The bend stays in the plane the limb already bends in, so an elbow never flips. Key the result as usual.

## Two copies of the same character

Entity names in a scene are unique, so a second copy of a model gets `mixamorig_hips_1` where its clips say `mixamorig:Hips`. Animation binds through the name the bone had before the editor made it unique, so both copies play their clips.
